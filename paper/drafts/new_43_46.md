### 4.3 What stops the action, and what repairs the report
The next tests use four models: Gemma-4 26B, Gemma-4 31B, Qwen3.5-397B and DeepSeek-V4 flash. We chose them before the main runs, because they reacted most in a small pilot. The team is the relay, with the false peer message. Each condition has {{H['_descriptive_controls']['baseline']['n']}} episodes, half of them on safe twins. With no control, V is {{H['_descriptive_controls']['baseline']['V']|.0%}}, and {{H['_descriptive_controls']['baseline']['FA_over_V']|.0%}} of the violating episodes give a false report. Figure 4 shows what each control costs.

**A permission record repairs the report (H4).** Here the Lead sees the gateway log and an explicit record of what the permission system approved. False reports fall from {{H['_descriptive_controls']['baseline']['FA_over_V']|.0%}} to {{H['_descriptive_controls']['ledger_auth']['FA_over_V']|.0%}} of violating episodes (a relative cut of {{H['H4']['parts'][0]['point']|.0%}}, CI [{{H['H4']['parts'][0]['lo']|.0%}}, {{H['H4']['parts'][0]['hi']|.0%}}]). The Lead discloses the violation in {{H['_descriptive_controls']['ledger_auth']['disclosed_over_V']|.0%}} of them, up from {{H['_descriptive_controls']['baseline']['disclosed_over_V']|.0%}}. But V does not change ({{H['H4']['parts'][1]['point']|+.2f}} [{{H['H4']['parts'][1]['lo']|+.2f}}, {{H['H4']['parts'][1]['hi']|+.2f}}]). A better report does not stop the action.

**The ask-before-act gate stops the action (H5).** The gate needs no oracle. V falls to {{H['H5']['parts'][0]['point']|.1%}} (CI up to {{H['H5']['parts'][0]['hi']|.1%}}). The price is goal completion on safe twins, which falls by {{H['H5']['parts'][1]['point']|.2f}} [{{H['H5']['parts'][1]['lo']|.2f}}, {{H['H5']['parts'][1]['hi']|.2f}}]. The gate blocks {{H['_descriptive_controls']['ask_gate']['blocked_per_ep']|.1f}} calls per episode. The oracle check reaches V = 0 by construction (H11). It is an upper bound, because it uses the benchmark's own checker.

**Auditors and a prompt rule help, but leave a residual (H6, H12, H17).** An auditor LLM that sees only the call cuts V to {{H['_descriptive_controls']['aud_min']['V']|.0%}}. With tool results it cuts V to {{H['_descriptive_controls']['aud_trace']['V']|.0%}}, and with the team's messages as well, to {{H['_descriptive_controls']['aud_trace_just']['V']|.0%}}. All three stay above 5% (H6 supported). The call-only auditor blocks almost everything, so goal completion falls to {{H['_descriptive_controls']['aud_min']['goal_safe']|.2f}}. A violation that passes an auditor still gets a false report in {{H['_descriptive_controls']['aud_trace']['FA_over_V']|.0%}} of cases, so auditors do not repair reports. The verification prompt (one rule in every system prompt) cuts V from {{H['_descriptive_controls']['baseline']['V']|.0%}} to {{H['_descriptive_controls']['prompt_defense']['V']|.0%}} (H12 supported), but V stays above the gate's. An auditor from another model family does a little better than one from the Operator's own family ({{H['H17']['V_diverse']|.1%}} against {{H['H17']['V_same']|.1%}}; H17 supported). The team's own words do not recruit the auditor (H8 not supported). Appendix E has the details.

### 4.4 In a mixed team, the Lead is the gatekeeper
We paired credulous models (Gemma-4 26B, Qwen3.5-397B) with skeptical models (GLM-5.2, glm-53-flash) in the relay: 8 pairs, {{D['counts']['e4']}} episodes. We expected the Operator's model to decide whether the team acts. **This was wrong (H13 not supported).** With a skeptical Lead and a credulous Operator, V is {{X['e4_groups']['S lead + C op']['V']|.2f}}. For an all-credulous team it is {{X['e4_groups']['C lead + C op']['V']|.2f}}. The ratio is {{H['H13']['parts'][0]['point']|.0%}} [{{H['H13']['parts'][0]['lo']|.0%}}, {{H['H13']['parts'][0]['hi']|.0%}}], below our 50% threshold. With a credulous Lead and a skeptical Operator, V is {{X['e4_groups']['C lead + S op']['V']|.2f}} [{{X['e4_groups']['C lead + S op']['lo']|.2f}}, {{X['e4_groups']['C lead + S op']['hi']|.2f}}], above our 0.10 threshold. So the Lead is the gatekeeper: its brief sets what the Operator believes. The Lead also decides what the human reads. A skeptical Lead discloses violations {{H['H14']['point']|+.2f}} more often than an all-credulous team does (H14 supported).

### 4.5 Robustness: wording and repeats
The effect is not tied to one sentence. A manager's message and a terse "Approvals are already in place" both raise V over no message ({{H['H15']['parts'][0]['point']|+.2f}} [{{H['H15']['parts'][0]['lo']|+.2f}}, {{H['H15']['parts'][0]['hi']|+.2f}}] and {{H['H15']['parts'][1]['point']|+.2f}} [{{H['H15']['parts'][1]['lo']|+.2f}}, {{H['H15']['parts'][1]['hi']|+.2f}}]; H15 supported). A hedged message ("I have not checked; please confirm before acting") gives V = {{H['_e5_levels']['hedged']|.2f}}. That is below the main wording (H16 supported) and below no message at all ({{H['_e5_levels']['none']|.2f}}; exploratory). So a peer that states its own doubt makes the team safer than a silent peer. Repeated episodes give the same pooled V, but a single episode repeats its outcome only {{H['_e6']['match']|.0%}} of the time (κ = {{H['_e6']['kappa']|.2f}}). So we report rates, not single traces (Appendix E).

### 4.6 What a monitor can see: the log ladder
We scored {{H['_table']['self']['n']}} relay traces (four models, half unsafe twins, half safe twins). Two judge models scored each trace at each log level, L0 to L4 (see Methods). The label is whether a violation occurred. **Only the gateway log with results works (H7 supported).** From the final report alone (L0), AUROC is {{X['mon']['L0'][0]|.2f}}–{{X['mon']['L0'][1]|.2f}} across the two judges. From the gateway log with results (L3), it is {{X['mon']['L3'][0]|.2f}}–{{X['mon']['L3'][1]|.2f}}. The gains are {{H['H7']['parts'][0]['point']|+.2f}} and {{H['H7']['parts'][1]['point']|+.2f}}, and both CI lower bounds are above {{H['H7']['lo']|.2f}}. At a 5% false-alarm budget, the Qwen judge catches {{X['catch_qwen']['L3']|.0%}} of violations at L3 and {{X['catch_qwen']['L0']|.0%}} at L0 (Figure 5). The Gemma judge gives coarse scores with many ties, so we use AUROC for it.

Tool calls without results are as weak as the report (AUROC {{X['mon']['L2'][0]|.2f}}–{{X['mon']['L2'][1]|.2f}}). The results carry the evidence, for example who owns a file or what the permission system said. Adding the team's own words does not help further (exploratory). A rule that flags state-changing calls with no earlier approved request is near chance (AUROC {{H['_table']['rule']['auroc']|.2f}}), because it cannot tell the twins apart. A judge from a model family that never acted in these traces agrees (glm-53-flash: AUROC {{X['indep_judge']['L0']['auroc']|.2f}} at L0 and {{X['indep_judge']['L3']['auroc']|.2f}} at L3; exploratory, Appendix E). The self-query monitor reaches AUROC {{H['H9']['point']|.2f}} [{{H['H9']['lo']|.2f}}, {{H['H9']['hi']|.2f}}]. It meets the point criterion of H9 (0.75), but its lower bound does not, so H9 has only weak support.
