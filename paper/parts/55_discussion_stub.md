## 5. Discussion and Limitations
**What the results say.** Acting without authorization, reporting it falsely and monitoring it are three different problems. A permission record repaired the report but not the action. An ask-before-act gate stopped the action at a cost in useful work. Only a log with tool results let a monitor see the failure. The mechanism test explains why a single sentence is enough: the claim does most of its harm because it displaces the agent's own check, and any reliable prompt to check restores it. @@E13DISC@@ That is also why the claim filter works. It does not decide whether a claim is true. It puts the question back.

**What a deployer should log and enforce.**
1. Write the log at the gateway, with tool results, where no agent can edit it. A monitor that reads the report ({{X['mon']['L0'][0]|.2f}}–{{X['mon']['L0'][1]|.2f}} AUROC) or the calls without results ({{X['mon']['L2'][0]|.2f}}–{{X['mon']['L2'][1]|.2f}}) is close to blind. One that reads calls with results reaches {{X['mon']['L3'][0]|.2f}}–{{X['mon']['L3'][1]|.2f}}.
2. Keep an explicit permission record, and make reports cite it and not a peer.
3. Enforce ask-before-act at the gateway. If you cannot, mark every approval claim that crosses an agent boundary as unverified (the claim filter), and do not rely on it alone, because a pressure message with no claim passes it.
4. Test your own models with matched twins first. Three of seven models hardly reacted to the claim and three reacted strongly, so model choice is a safety decision.

**Predictions that did not hold.** The Operator's model does not decide alone, because the Lead is a gatekeeper (Section 4.4). The team's own words do not recruit an auditor or a monitor (Section 4.3). A short attacker search found no message stronger than the scripted one (Section 4.5). We did not show that the claim filter costs less useful work than the gate (Section 4.8). Appendix C lists all preregistered predictions by experiment.

**Limitations.** Appendix A has the full list. We used open-weight models only. The attackers are weak, since they are scripted wordings and short searches. The tools are mock tools, and the oracle check is an upper bound. The judges come from the same model families as the agents. The experiments of Sections 4.7 and 4.8 are small, because the API rate limit fell during the sprint, and one planned test (a stealthy Operator) was not run. We assume the gateway log cannot be edited by agents, but real agents have edited logs [13]. **Future work:** frontier models, trained attackers, real swarm logs and mesh topologies.
