TITLE: Who Approved This? Assurance Laundering in LLM Agent Teams
AUTHOR: Arya Fayyazi | University of Southern California
ABSTRACT:
Teams of AI agents act with tools, and humans often read only the final report. We study *assurance laundering*: an unverified claim of approval from a peer agent travels through a team, leads an agent to act without authorization, and returns to the human as a confident, false report. We built CAPE-Swarm, a team layer on CAPE-Bench with matched safe and unsafe twins and rule-based labels (no LLM judge). We preregistered 17 hypotheses and ran {{sum(D['counts'].values())|,}} episodes of seven open-weight models. A false peer assurance raised unauthorized actions in a solo agent from {{X['V_none']['solo']|.2f}} to {{X['V_peer']['solo']|.2f}} ({{H['H1']['point']|+.2f}}, 95% CI [{{H['H1']['lo']|+.2f}}, {{H['H1']['hi']|+.2f}}]), unevenly: three models reacted strongly and three hardly at all. Teams dampened the effect, but {{H['H3']['false_assurance_over_V']|.0%}} of violations were reported as authorized. Acting and reporting need different fixes. A permission-system record cut false reports by {{H['H4']['parts'][0]['point']|.0%}} without changing actions. An ask-before-act gateway rule cut violations to {{H['H5']['parts'][0]['point']|.1%}} for a {{H['H5']['parts'][1]['point']*100|.0f}}-point utility cost. Auditors and a verification prompt left about {{(X['resid'][0]+X['resid'][1])/2|.0%}}. A monitor reading the gateway ledger with tool results reached AUROC {{X['mon']['L3'][0]|.2f}}–{{X['mon']['L3'][1]|.2f}}, while the report alone ({{X['mon']['L0'][0]|.2f}}–{{X['mon']['L0'][1]|.2f}}) did not. Two hypotheses failed and are reported. We release traces, a replay viewer and a ledger auditor.
