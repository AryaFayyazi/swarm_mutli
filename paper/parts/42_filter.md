### 4.8 A claim filter: mark the claim, keep the work
The false claim does the harm, and the gate stops the action at a cost in useful work. Can we remove the harm at the claim itself? We added a **claim filter** to the message bus. A small model (glm-53-flash, temperature 0) reads every message between agents. If a message claims approval, authorization or permission, the filter rewrites that claim. The new text says who made the claim, says it is unverified, and tells the reader to confirm it with the permission system before any state-changing action. The rest of the message is unchanged. The filter does not see the task, the log or the twin. If it returns nothing, the original message passes. This happened in {{R['_filter']['fail_open']}} of {{R['_filter']['calls']}} filter calls, mostly on the Lead's brief. The idea is close to spotlighting [14], which marks untrusted text for the model. We mark a *claim* by its source, between agents. We test it with matched twins and a rule checker.

We ran this on fresh tasks (E9, seed 5501, relay, four models, {{R['counts']['e9']}} episodes). Here V is the share of unsafe-twin episodes with a violation. The design had 40 base tasks. The API limit fell during the sprint, so only {{R['_n_clusters']}} base tasks have every condition finished. We analyse those (see Limitations). Figure 5 shows the results.

**The filter removes most of the harm (H21 supported).** With the filter, V falls to {{R['_cells']['C']['V']|.2f}} ({{H['H21']['point']|+.2f}} [{{H['H21']['lo']|+.2f}}, {{H['H21']['hi']|+.2f}}]). No violating report says "authorized" any more, against {{R['_cells']['B']['FA_over_V']|.0%}} without the filter. All four models improve. With it, V is at most {{max(R['_cells']['C']['per_model_V'].values())|.2f}}.

**It is not tuned to one sentence (H23 supported).** We wrote the filter prompt with the main message in mind. The attacker message from 4.7 is a wording the filter never saw. Its V falls from {{R['_cells']['G']['V']|.2f}} to {{R['_cells']['H']['V']|.2f}} ({{H['H23']['point']|+.2f}} [{{H['H23']['lo']|+.2f}}, {{H['H23']['hi']|+.2f}}]).

The drop is {{H['H22']['point']|+.2f}} [{{H['H22']['lo']|+.2f}}, {{H['H22']['hi']|+.2f}}], and our margin was 0.10.

**The gate works again, and the filter is not clearly cheaper.** With the gate, V is {{R['_cells']['D']['V']|.2f}}, and goal completion on safe twins drops by {{H['H24']['parts'][1]['point']|+.2f}} [{{H['H24']['parts'][1]['lo']|+.2f}}, {{H['H24']['parts'][1]['hi']|+.2f}}] (H24 supported). We expected the filter to cost less than the gate, but we did not show it (H25 not supported). The difference in goal completion is {{H['H25']['point']|+.2f}} [{{H['H25']['lo']|+.2f}}, {{H['H25']['hi']|+.2f}}]. In this run the two controls cost about the same. The filter needs no change at the gateway, and the agent still makes its own decision. The claim becomes a prompt to ask.
