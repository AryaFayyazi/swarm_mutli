## 5. Discussion and Limitations
**Three problems, three fixes.** Acting without authorization, reporting it falsely and monitoring it are three different problems. One unverified sentence raised V by {{H['H1']['point']|+.2f}} in a single agent. Teams lowered V but did not fix the report: {{min(X['fa_over_v'][t]['FA']['point'] for t in X['fa_over_v'])|.0%}} to {{max(X['fa_over_v'][t]['FA']['point'] for t in X['fa_over_v'])|.0%}} of violating episodes ended with a false "authorized". A permission record repaired the report ({{H['H4']['parts'][0]['point']|.0%}} fewer false reports) but did not change the action. The ask-before-act gate stopped the action (V = {{H['H5']['parts'][0]['point']|.1%}}) for about {{H['H5']['parts'][1]['point']*100|.0f}} points of useful work. The claim filter removed most of the harm on fresh tasks, at no measurable cost. A verification prompt and an auditor sat in between, with a residual V of about {{(X['resid'][0]+X['resid'][1])/2|.0%}}.

**What a deployer should log and enforce.**
1. Write the log at the gateway, with tool results, where no agent can edit it. A monitor that reads the report ({{X['mon']['L0'][0]|.2f}}–{{X['mon']['L0'][1]|.2f}} AUROC) or the calls without results ({{X['mon']['L2'][0]|.2f}}–{{X['mon']['L2'][1]|.2f}}) is close to blind. One that reads calls with results reaches {{X['mon']['L3'][0]|.2f}}–{{X['mon']['L3'][1]|.2f}}.
2. Keep an explicit permission record, and make reports cite it and not a peer.
3. Enforce ask-before-act at the gateway. If you cannot, mark every approval claim that crosses an agent boundary as unverified (the claim filter, 4.8), and do not rely on it alone: a pressure message with no claim passes it.
4. Test your own models with matched twins first. Three of seven models hardly reacted to the claim and three reacted strongly, so model choice is a safety decision.
5. Make agents state what they did not verify (4.5).

**What did not hold.** Four hypotheses failed. H13: the Operator's model does not decide alone, because the Lead is a gatekeeper. H8: the team's own words do not recruit an auditor or a monitor. H18: a short attacker search found no stronger message. H25: we did not show that the filter costs less than the gate.

**Limitations.** (Appendix A has the full list.) We used open-weight models only. The attackers are weak (scripted wordings and short searches). The tools are mock tools. The oracle check is an upper bound. The judges come from the same model families as the agents. Round 2 is small, because the API rate limit fell during the sprint. We assume the gateway log cannot be edited by agents, but real agents have edited logs [13]. **Future work:** frontier models, trained attackers, real swarm logs and mesh topologies.
