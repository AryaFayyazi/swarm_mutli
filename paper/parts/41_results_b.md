### 4.4 In a mixed team, the Lead is the gatekeeper
We paired credulous models (Gemma-4 26B, Qwen3.5-397B) and skeptical models (GLM-5.2, GLM-5.3 flash) in the relay: 8 cross pairs, {{D['counts']['e4']}} episodes. We expected that the Operator's model would decide whether the team acts. **This was wrong (H13 not supported).** A skeptical Lead in front of a credulous Operator keeps only {{H['H13']['parts'][0]['point']|.0%}} of the all-credulous violation rate (violations {{X['e4_groups']['S lead + C op']['V']|.2f}} against {{X['e4_groups']['C lead + C op']['V']|.2f}}; 95% CI of the ratio [{{H['H13']['parts'][0]['lo']|.0%}}, {{H['H13']['parts'][0]['hi']|.0%}}], below our 50% threshold). A credulous Lead in front of a skeptical Operator gives {{X['e4_groups']['C lead + S op']['V']|.2f}} [{{X['e4_groups']['C lead + S op']['lo']|.2f}}, {{X['e4_groups']['C lead + S op']['hi']|.2f}}], above our 0.10 threshold and above the all-skeptical team ({{X['e4_groups']['S lead + S op']['V']|.2f}}). The Lead is a gatekeeper: the brief it writes sets what the Operator believes, so a careful Lead removes most of a credulous Operator's violations, and a careless Lead leaks some risk to a careful Operator. The Lead also decides what the human reads. Among violating episodes, a skeptical Lead's report discloses the violation {{H['H14']['point']|+.2f}} more often than an all-credulous team's [{{H['H14']['lo']|+.2f}}, {{H['H14']['hi']|+.2f}}] (H14 supported).
