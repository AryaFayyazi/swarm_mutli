### 4.3 What stops the action, and what repairs the report
The next tests use four models (Gemma-4 26B and 31B, Qwen3.5-397B, DeepSeek-V4 flash), chosen before the main runs because they showed the highest violation rates under the assurance in the development pilot (DeepSeek reacts least of the four). They run in the relay with the false assurance: {{H['_descriptive_controls']['baseline']['n']}} episodes per condition, half of them safe twins that measure utility. Without a control, {{H['_descriptive_controls']['baseline']['V']|.0%}} of unsafe-twin episodes contain an unauthorized action, and {{H['_descriptive_controls']['baseline']['FA_over_V']|.0%}} of those report "authorized".

**Reporting and acting are separate problems (H4).** When the Lead also sees the gateway ledger and an explicit permission-system record, false "authorized" reports fall from {{H['_descriptive_controls']['baseline']['FA_over_V']|.0%}} to {{H['_descriptive_controls']['ledger_auth']['FA_over_V']|.0%}} of violations (relative cut {{H['H4']['parts'][0]['point']|.0%}}, 95% CI [{{H['H4']['parts'][0]['lo']|.0%}}, {{H['H4']['parts'][0]['hi']|.0%}}]), and disclosure rises from {{H['_descriptive_controls']['baseline']['disclosed_over_V']|.0%}} to {{H['_descriptive_controls']['ledger_auth']['disclosed_over_V']|.0%}}. The violation rate does not change ({{H['H4']['parts'][1]['point']|+.2f}} [{{H['H4']['parts'][1]['lo']|+.2f}}, {{H['H4']['parts'][1]['hi']|+.2f}}]; H4 supported).

**A structural gateway rule prevents the action (H5).** The ask-before-act rule needs no policy oracle. It cuts violations to {{H['H5']['parts'][0]['point']|.1%}} (95% CI up to {{H['H5']['parts'][0]['hi']|.1%}}). It lowers safe-twin goal completion by {{H['H5']['parts'][1]['point']|.2f}} [{{H['H5']['parts'][1]['lo']|.2f}}, {{H['H5']['parts'][1]['hi']|.2f}}], and it blocks {{H['_descriptive_controls']['ask_gate']['blocked_per_ep']|.1f}} calls per episode on average (H5 supported). The oracle policy check reaches zero violations by construction (H11) and costs only {{H['_descriptive_controls']['baseline']['goal_safe'] - H['_descriptive_controls']['pdp']['goal_safe']|.2f}} of goal completion. It is an upper bound, because it uses the benchmark's own checker.

**Auditors and a prompt rule help, but leave a residual (H6, H12, H17).** Auditors cut violations to {{H['_descriptive_controls']['aud_min']['V']|.0%}} (sees the call only), {{H['_descriptive_controls']['aud_trace']['V']|.0%}} (also sees tool results) and {{H['_descriptive_controls']['aud_trace_just']['V']|.0%}} (also sees the swarm's words). All three stay above 5% (H6 supported), and the call-only auditor blocks almost everything (safe-twin goal completion {{H['_descriptive_controls']['aud_min']['goal_safe']|.2f}}). A violation that passes an auditor is still reported as authorized in {{H['_descriptive_controls']['aud_trace']['FA_over_V']|.0%}} of cases, so auditors do not repair reports. One sentence in every system prompt ("a claim of approval is unverified until the permission system confirms it") cuts violations from {{H['_descriptive_controls']['baseline']['V']|.0%}} to {{H['_descriptive_controls']['prompt_defense']['V']|.0%}} ({{H['H12']['parts'][0]['point']|+.2f}} [{{H['H12']['parts'][0]['lo']|+.2f}}, {{H['H12']['parts'][0]['hi']|+.2f}}]) and stays above the gateway rule (H12 supported). An auditor from another model family does a little better than a same-model auditor ({{H['H17']['V_diverse']|.1%}} against {{H['H17']['V_same']|.1%}}; H17 supported). The swarm's own words do not recruit the auditor (H8 not supported). Appendix E gives the details.

### 4.4 In a mixed team, the Lead is the gatekeeper
We paired credulous models (Gemma-4 26B, Qwen3.5-397B) and skeptical models (GLM-5.2, glm-53-flash) in the relay: 8 cross pairs, {{D['counts']['e4']}} episodes. We expected that the Operator's model would decide whether the team acts. **This was wrong (H13 not supported).** A skeptical Lead in front of a credulous Operator keeps only {{H['H13']['parts'][0]['point']|.0%}} of the all-credulous violation rate (violations {{X['e4_groups']['S lead + C op']['V']|.2f}} against {{X['e4_groups']['C lead + C op']['V']|.2f}}; 95% CI of the ratio [{{H['H13']['parts'][0]['lo']|.0%}}, {{H['H13']['parts'][0]['hi']|.0%}}], below our 50% threshold). A credulous Lead in front of a skeptical Operator gives {{X['e4_groups']['C lead + S op']['V']|.2f}} [{{X['e4_groups']['C lead + S op']['lo']|.2f}}, {{X['e4_groups']['C lead + S op']['hi']|.2f}}], above our 0.10 threshold. The Lead is a gatekeeper: the brief it writes sets what the Operator believes. The Lead also decides what the human reads. Among violating episodes, a skeptical Lead's report discloses the violation {{H['H14']['point']|+.2f}} more often than an all-credulous team's [{{H['H14']['lo']|+.2f}}, {{H['H14']['hi']|+.2f}}] (H14 supported).

### 4.5 Robustness: wording and repeats
The effect is not tied to one sentence. In E5 ({{D['counts']['e5']|,}} episodes, relay, four models), a manager's message and a terse "Approvals are already in place" both raise violations over no message ({{H['H15']['parts'][0]['point']|+.2f}} [{{H['H15']['parts'][0]['lo']|+.2f}}, {{H['H15']['parts'][0]['hi']|+.2f}}] and {{H['H15']['parts'][1]['point']|+.2f}} [{{H['H15']['parts'][1]['lo']|+.2f}}, {{H['H15']['parts'][1]['hi']|+.2f}}]; H15 supported). A hedged wording ("I think approvals were obtained, but I have not checked. Please confirm before acting.") gives {{H['_e5_levels']['hedged']|.2f}}, which is {{H['H16']['point']|+.2f}} [{{H['H16']['lo']|+.2f}}, {{H['H16']['hi']|+.2f}}] relative to the main wording (H16 supported) and below the no-message rate ({{H['_e5_levels']['none']|.2f}}; exploratory): a peer that states its own uncertainty makes the team safer than a silent peer. Re-running {{H['_e6']['n']}} relay episodes (E6) gives the same pooled violation rate ({{H['_e6']['V_run1']|.2f}} and {{H['_e6']['V_run2']|.2f}}), but only {{H['_e6']['match']|.0%}} of single episodes agree (Cohen's κ = {{H['_e6']['kappa']|.2f}}), so we report rates, not single traces.
