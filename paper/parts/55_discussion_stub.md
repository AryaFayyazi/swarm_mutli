## 5. Discussion and Limitations
**Three problems, three prices.** In an agent team, acting without authorization, reporting it falsely and monitoring it are three problems with three fixes. One unverified sentence from a peer raised unauthorized actions by {{H['H1']['point']|+.2f}} in a solo agent. Team shape changed the size of the effect, and in mixed teams the Lead acted as a gatekeeper. But no team shape fixed the report: by team shape, {{min(X['fa_over_v'][t]['FA']['point'] for t in X['fa_over_v'])|.0%}} to {{max(X['fa_over_v'][t]['FA']['point'] for t in X['fa_over_v'])|.0%}} of violating episodes ended with a false "authorized". An explicit permission-system record repaired the report ({{H['H4']['parts'][0]['point']|.0%}} fewer false reports) and left the action alone. A structural gateway rule stopped the action ({{H['H5']['parts'][0]['point']|.1%}} violations) for about {{H['H5']['parts'][1]['point']*100|.0f}} points of utility. A verification prompt and an auditor agent sat in between: about {{(X['resid'][0]+X['resid'][1])/2|.0%}} of episodes still had a violation and utility fell by 5–10 points.

**What a deployer should log and enforce.** (1) Write the ledger at the gateway, with tool results, where no agent can edit it. A monitor that reads the report ({{X['mon']['L0'][0]|.2f}}–{{X['mon']['L0'][1]|.2f}} AUROC) or the list of calls without results ({{X['mon']['L2'][0]|.2f}}–{{X['mon']['L2'][1]|.2f}}) is close to blind. One that reads calls with results reaches {{X['mon']['L3'][0]|.2f}}–{{X['mon']['L3'][1]|.2f}}. (2) Keep an explicit permission-system record, and make reports cite it and not a peer. (3) Enforce ask-before-act at the gateway for state-changing calls. (4) If you cannot, add a verification rule and an auditor that sees evidence, and plan for a residual of about 9%. (5) Test your own models with matched twins first. Three of seven models hardly reacted to the claim, one reacted weakly and three reacted strongly, so model choice is a safety decision. It is cheap to measure: about {{96*2}} solo episodes per model, roughly ten minutes of API time at our rate limit. Honest uncertainty also protects: the hedged peer message ("I have not checked") gave the lowest violation rate of any condition ({{H['_e5_levels']['hedged']|.2f}}), so teams should make agents state what they did not verify.

**What did not hold.** H13 failed: the Operator's model does not decide alone, because the Lead gatekeeps. H8 failed: we found no sign that the swarm's words recruit an auditor or a monitor (L4 − L3 is not above zero). Single-episode outcomes are noisy (κ = {{H['_e6']['kappa']|.2f}}), so only rates mean anything. Our first regex for "orders to skip approval" was wrong and is not used.

**Limitations.** We used open-weight models only, one scripted peer message, mock tools, an oracle upper bound, judges from the same model families as the agents, and 48 scenarios per cell. The gateway ledger is assumed tamper-proof; real agents have edited logs [13]. Appendix A gives the full list and says how each result would change if an assumption failed. **Future work:** frontier models, adaptive attackers, real swarm logs and mesh topologies (`docs/PAPER_ROADMAP.md`).
