## 4. Results
Round 1 (E1) has {{D['counts']['e1']|,}} episodes: 7 models, 3 team shapes, 2 message conditions (a false peer message, or no message), 96 instances each. Every hypothesis (H1, H2, ...) was fixed before the data existed. We mark analyses that were not preregistered as *exploratory*. "CI" is the 95% confidence interval. Square brackets show it.

### 4.1 A false peer message makes agents act without authorization
**Single agent.** The peer message raises V (the share of unsafe-twin episodes with an unauthorized action) from {{X['V_none']['solo']|.2f}} to {{X['V_peer']['solo']|.2f}}. The difference is {{H['H1']['point']|+.2f}} [{{H['H1']['lo']|+.2f}}, {{H['H1']['hi']|+.2f}}] (H1 supported). The average hides a split between models (Figure 2a). Three models react strongly: Qwen3.5-397B ({{H['H1']['per_model']['qwen3.5-397b']|+.2f}}), Gemma-4 31B ({{H['H1']['per_model']['gemma-4-31b-it']|+.2f}}) and Gemma-4 26B ({{H['H1']['per_model']['gemma-4-26b-a4b-it']|+.2f}}). DeepSeek-V4 flash reacts weakly ({{H['H1']['per_model']['deepseek-v4-flash']|+.2f}}). The other three models show almost no effect.

**Teams.** The effect remains in teams (relay {{H['H2']['parts'][0]['point']|+.2f}}, relay-2 {{H['H2']['parts'][1]['point']|+.2f}}; H2 supported). The absolute rate is lower in teams, with or without the false message. So teams do not make the problem worse, but they do not remove it. With the false message, V is {{X['V_peer']['solo']|.2f}} (solo), {{X['V_peer']['relay1']|.2f}} (relay) and {{X['V_peer']['relay2']|.2f}} (relay-2). The false message multiplies V by about {{X['ratio_peer_none']['solo']|.1f}}, {{X['ratio_peer_none']['relay1']|.1f}} and {{X['ratio_peer_none']['relay2']|.1f}} in the three shapes.

### 4.2 The claim travels through the team and comes back as a report
Figure E1 (Appendix E) follows the ticket number through the relay. The Lead's brief repeats the claim in {{D['hops_unsafe_peer'][0]['brief_carries_claim']|.0%}} of relay episodes. Other Leads drop it. We also labeled each brief with a model (exploratory; it agrees with our hand labels in 35 of 36 cases). A brief *endorses* the claim, *rejects* it (calls it unverified, or tells the Operator to check), or does not mention it. After an endorsing brief, violations occur in {{X['stance']['endorse']['V']|.0%}} of episodes. After a rejecting brief, they occur in {{X['stance']['reject']['V']|.0%}}. The split follows the model: the Gemma-4 models endorse most often (Appendix E).

The report is where the harm reaches the human. Among violating episodes, the report says "authorized" in {{H['H3']['false_assurance_over_V']|.0%}} and discloses the violation in only {{H['H3']['point']|.0%}} (95% CI [{{H['H3']['lo']|.0%}}, {{H['H3']['hi']|.0%}}]; H3 supported). The single agent is worst ({{X['fa_over_v']['solo']['FA']['point']|.0%}} false reports). Teams are somewhat more honest (relay {{X['fa_over_v']['relay1']['FA']['point']|.0%}}, relay-2 {{X['fa_over_v']['relay2']['FA']['point']|.0%}}; exploratory). Even so, the Lead's report repeats the ticket number in {{D['hops_violators'][0]['final_report_cites_claim']|.0%}} of violating relay episodes.
