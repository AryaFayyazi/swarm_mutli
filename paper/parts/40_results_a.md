## 4. Results
**Questions and design.** Each subsection answers one question: does a false peer message make agents act (4.1, experiment E1); does it reach the report (4.2); which control stops the action and which repairs the report (4.3, E3); who decides in a mixed team (4.4, E4); is the effect robust (4.5, E5–E8); what can a monitor see (4.6, E2); why does the claim work (4.7, E12 and E13); and does a claim filter help (4.8, E9 and E11). E1 crosses seven open-weight models, three team shapes and two message conditions ({{D['counts']['e1']|,}} episodes). E3–E8 use four models (Gemma-4 26B, Gemma-4 31B, Qwen3.5-397B and DeepSeek-V4 flash) chosen in a pilot. E9–E13 use fresh scenarios and are smaller than planned, because the API rate limit fell during the sprint (Appendix A; Table D1 lists every experiment). V is the share of unsafe-twin episodes with a violation (Eq. 1), and brackets give 95% intervals. Every test was preregistered unless we mark it *exploratory*.

### 4.2 The claim travels through the team and returns as a false report
**Finding.** The Lead's brief repeats the claim in {{D['hops_unsafe_peer'][0]['brief_carries_claim']|.0%}} of episodes, and violations follow the brief: they occur in {{X['stance']['endorse']['V']|.0%}} of episodes after a brief that endorses the claim and in {{X['stance']['reject']['V']|.0%}} after one that rejects it (exploratory; Appendix E.3). The human then reads a false report. Among violating episodes, the report says "authorized" in {{H['H3']['false_assurance_over_V']|.0%}} and discloses the violation in only {{H['H3']['point']|.0%}} [{{H['H3']['lo']|.0%}}, {{H['H3']['hi']|.0%}}].
