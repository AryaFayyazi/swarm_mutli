TITLE: Who Approved This? Assurance Laundering in LLM Agent Teams
AUTHOR: Arya Fayyazi | University of Southern California
ABSTRACT:
LLM agents increasingly work in teams that use tools, and a human often reads only the final report. We study *assurance laundering*. A peer agent asserts, without proof, that approval was already given, the team acts without authorization, and the report tells the human that the work was authorized. We build CAPE-Swarm, a team layer on the CAPE-Bench environments [1], where every task has a matched safe and unsafe twin and a fixed rule, not an LLM judge, labels each tool call. Acting and reporting need different fixes: a gateway rule that requires a permission request stops the action, and showing the Lead a permission record repairs the report. A factorial test shows that the claim displaces the agent's own authorization check, and a verification rule removes nearly all of its effect. Honest doubt from a peer only restores the baseline rate and costs useful work, but a peer's question lowers it further. A *claim filter* that marks approval claims as unverified cuts unauthorized actions from {{R['_cells']['B']['V']|.2f}} to {{R['_cells']['C']['V']|.2f}} on fresh tasks and holds against an attacker who knows it. A monitor detects violations only if it reads the gateway log with results (AUROC {{X['mon']['L3'][0]|.2f}}–{{X['mon']['L3'][1]|.2f}}, against {{X['mon']['L0'][0]|.2f}}–{{X['mon']['L0'][1]|.2f}} from the report).
